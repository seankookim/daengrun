**R16 — `intent_missing` is a bare payments-row existence check, so a booking with any unrelated payments row is never repaired.**

**R17 — `late_booking_sweep` is a FUNCTION, so it holds a `FOR UPDATE` lock on every booking it touches for the whole batch; a handoff stamp then lands on a row the clock already closed.**
`0117:965-966`, `0117:466-468`, `0117:977`

`language plpgsql` on a function means no `commit` between rows; `lock_timeout` 2000ms bounds how long the sweep **waits**, not how long it **holds**.

*Concrete failure:* an 11:03 tick with 30 expired check-ins. At 11:03:03 the resolver sees zero stamps on booking X, writes `no_show`, and holds X's lock until the batch commits at 11:03:09. At 11:03:04 the runner, holding the dog, taps 인계확인; the stamp UPDATE blocks, then lands on a row that now says `no_show` — verified that `enforce_booking_transition` returns early when `old.status = new.status` (0066:41), so nothing refuses a handoff stamp on a terminal row. The promotion 409s and the durable record says the dog was never picked up while the runner is holding it. Every owner cancel, `start_run` and `answer_checkin` on the other 29 bookings stalls for the batch. The batch does not create the race; it widens it from ~1ms to ~6s.

*Cheapest fix:* the cheaper half first — give `confirm_handoff` a status gate (`.eq('status', …)` on the stamp write, or a `before update` guard refusing a handoff stamp on a terminal booking). Then convert the sweep to a `procedure` with a `commit` per iteration (`pg_cron` can `call` it).

## 3. Contradictions of Sean's rulings

**Eight, not three.** Each is a first-class conflict where the code produces an outcome a ruling forbids:

1. **R2** — *NO SILENT RUNNER-PAY CUT* and **D3**. A timer freezes a live post-custody run into `incident_review`, a terminal with no marketplace money exit; the runner walks the dog and can never be paid, and the only thing either party is told is a dog-welfare push. The file's own citation of 0068 (*"a timer may flip status, never money"*, 0117:56-58) is defeated because here the status flip **is** the money decision.
2. **R5** — *NO SILENT RUNNER-PAY CUT*, and §9's own encoding of Sean's 2026-08-21 ruling at 0117:1145-1151. The ceiling arm `no_show`s a booking carrying `arrived_at`, removing the runner's only compensation path — the exact strip the carve-out four hundred lines away was written to prevent.
3. **R6** — *NO SILENT RUNNER-PAY CUT*. An owner's check-in answer is a cheaper exit than the cancel button, taking the runner from ₩12,450 to ₩0 through a path the runner is never told exists.
4. **R7** — **D5**, *"a timer must not overrule a human's recorded statement."* One side's recorded 진행하겠습니다 is voided at T+1h on the strength of the other side's silence.
5. **R9** — **D5** again, in the sharpest form: two 'proceeding' statements that explicitly renewed the deadline are overruled by the tick that was already in flight.
6. **R8** — *grace 30 / ceiling 3 hours.* For every booking the step-aside closes, the ruled ceiling never fires at all — maximum lateness is silently infinite, and the booking never reaches a terminal.
7. **R17** — **D3**, *"no_show may never be written post-custody."* The batch-wide lock lets a handoff stamp land on a row the clock closed as `no_show`; the runner holds the dog and is told the booking is over.
8. **R11** — *THE SILENT-STALEMATE RULE* and **D4**. On the 10% tier the wronged owner pays ₩2,490 and the absent runner is paid ₩1,245 — nobody-pays-nobody-is-paid, inverted. Unlike the other seven this is unchanged trunk behaviour that 0117 consciously declined to fix (0117:1198-1201), which is why it is a scope escalation rather than a regression.

R12 (disclosure) and R13 (km on the silent path) are arguable readings of *no silent runner-pay cut* and the stalemate rule respectively; both are in §4 rather than counted here.

## 4. What needs Sean

1. **Does the ceiling waiver apply to the 10% `confirmed` tier (R11)?** Today a booking 5 hours past start with no departure, no arrival and no stamps charges the owner 10% and pays half of it to the absent runner. The slice deliberately left this open. **Recommend: yes — hoist `cancel_moves_no_money` above the tier split**, so a recorded runner fault or a past-ceiling booking with no evidence quotes 0 on every arm. It is one `case` reshuffle in `marketplace_cancel_fee` and makes arm ① reachable on the tier where it matters most.
2. **Does the stalemate rule cover granted km (R13)?** As written, the silent path loses the 72-hour lifetime grace and the spoken path keeps it — the owner is penalised precisely where nobody spoke. **Recommend: drop the restore for `ceiling`/`ceiling_backfill`/`void` and let 0075 own the netting rule**, keeping the file's own "one copy of the netting rule" principle; the timer-created-value concern is thin when the alternative is destroying ₩15,000 of an owner's km for a runner's no-show.
3. **What does the runner get told when the ceiling waives their 50% (R12)?** The reduction is Sean's own ruling, but the runner's push currently says only *보호자가 예약을 취소했어요*. **Recommend: name the rule in the runner's copy** (3+ hours past start, no arrival recorded, no fee charged and therefore no compensation) and give §6b a client entry point — it has zero call sites today, so the door Sean opened for after-the-fact statements does not exist in the app.
4. **§4.2 remains unruled, and R10 means the record it will be applied to is currently mis-attributed** — a runner reporting the owner's no-show is filed as `party='runner'`, immutably. **Recommend: land R10's one-line `source` filter before the flag flips**, so no statements are collected under the wrong axis in the interim; the ruling itself can wait.
